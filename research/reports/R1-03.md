## Full report as delivered

RESEARCH REPORT — Second-hand/used/refurbished commerce: seller & marketplace-operator problems (JAPAC focus, US/EU comparison)

A. CASUAL SELLERS (decluttering)
- No-show/ghosting: vendor-published data (ShowdUp.com, Jan 2026, self-serving — LOW CONFIDENCE, no public methodology beyond "50,000 appointments" claim) states 70% of confirmed Facebook Marketplace buyers don't show up; top reasons are "forgot" (38%) and "found better deal" (24%); non-refundable deposits cut no-shows by 82%. https://www.showdup.com/blog/facebook-marketplace-no-show-statistics
- Effort/donation trade-off: informal survey of declutterers (Clutter Fairy, Houston, 2025 — LOW CONFIDENCE, small qualitative sample) shows people default to donating over selling because listing/selling "requires more energy," with no quantified sell-through or time-to-sell data. https://www.clutterfairyhouston.com/survey-288-results-a-personal-review-of-2025-decluttering-and-organizing/
- Lowballing and lowball-offer complaints are described across seller forums/blogs as the most universal seller frustration on Facebook Marketplace/OfferUp, but no rigorous quantified study was found (anecdotal/aggregator content only — LOW CONFIDENCE). https://maggiemcgaugh.com/blog/lowmarketplaceoffers ; https://www.showdup.com/blog/how-to-stop-no-shows-marketplace
- GAP: no credible, methodologically transparent statistic found for "share of listings that never sell" or median time-to-sell across major P2P marketplaces (eBay/Facebook Marketplace/OfferUp/Mercari) after multiple search attempts — this is a real evidence gap, flag as an open research question for the hackathon team.
- Pandemic-era baseline (MagnifyMoney/LendingTree 2020-21, dated but real survey; page 403'd on fetch so treat as search-summary only, MEDIUM CONFIDENCE): ~40% of Americans sold something online, averaging $1,810. https://www.magnifymoney.com/news/selling-online-pandemic-survey/

B. PROFESSIONAL RESELLERS / SMALL REFURBISHERS
- Crosslisting economics (vendor/blog sources, MEDIUM-LOW CONFIDENCE — commercial crosslisting-tool sites): manual crosslisting takes 10–15 min/item/platform; sellers on 3+ platforms report up to 180% higher sell-through than single-platform sellers. https://closo.co/blogs/closo-comparison/cross-listing-poshmark-in-2025-7-best-tools-to-scale-your-sales-faster
- Poshmark fee structure (2025, MEDIUM CONFIDENCE, aggregator but fee facts verifiable): flat $2.95 fee under $15, 20% commission at/above $15 — materially higher than eBay's ~13% blended fee — forcing resellers to target 100–300% markups over sourcing cost to cover fees, time and storage. https://closo.co/blogs/fees/poshmark-seller-fee-2025-complete-guide-to-payouts-fees-profit-margins
- Refurbisher grading/QA at scale: Back Market grades devices Fair/Good/Excellent/Premium, requires 25+ functional checks and 80%+ battery health minimum, and continuously tracks refurbishers on defect rate, return rate and response time, delisting those below threshold (2025 data, MEDIUM CONFIDENCE — aggregator, not Back Market's own filing). https://thebrandhopper.com/featured-startups/back-market-founders-business-model-revenue-streams-funding/
- Working capital burden is starkest in India's inventory-led used-car resale model: Spinny's procurement (sourcing+refurbishment) cost was ~80% of total expenses (₹4,304 Cr of total spend in FY25), and Cars24's car procurement was 81% of total expenses (₹5,555 Cr), versus CarTrade's asset-light marketplace model posting an 85% profit surge in FY25 — direct evidence that owning inventory/condition risk is what drains reseller/refurbisher economics. Sources (HIGH CONFIDENCE, verified financial press): https://inc42.com/features/spinnys-pre-ipo-financial-turnaround-india-used-car-market-churn-profits/ ; https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/cars24-reports-10-revenue-decline-in-fy25-losses-rise-marginally-11160853

C. MARKETPLACE OPERATORS
- Cars24 (India, FY25, HIGH CONFIDENCE — press citing filings): gross revenue fell 10% to ₹6,233 Cr; net loss rose 9% to ₹543 Cr; ROCE -21.1%, EBITDA margin -6.8%; spent ₹1.11 to earn ₹1 of operating revenue — a clear illustration of why full-stack/inventory used-goods models struggle. https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/cars24-reports-10-revenue-decline-in-fy25-losses-rise-marginally-11160853
- Spinny (India, FY25, HIGH CONFIDENCE): revenue +25% to ₹4,657 Cr, losses narrowed 28% to ₹424 Cr but procurement remained ~80% of costs — losses shrinking mainly via marketing/opex cuts, not unit economics fix. https://inc42.com/features/spinnys-pre-ipo-financial-turnaround-india-used-car-market-churn-profits/
- Mercari (Japan/US, FY2025, MEDIUM CONFIDENCE — trade press, not the primary filing which I could not get readable text from): US Marketplace GMV fell 27% YoY in a recent quarter; a September 2024 flat 10% buyer fee "hit particularly hard," prompting a January 2025 split buyer/seller fee redesign; ongoing fraud/phishing problems in both Japan and the US required new security measures. https://www.valueaddedresource.net/mercari-aims-to-grow-gmv-increase-security-us-still-struggles/
- Carousell (JAPAC, 2025 Regional Scam Trends Report, HIGH CONFIDENCE — primary source, self-reported): even at claimed 99.96% scam-free transaction rate, the company still suspended 451,000+ accounts and reviewed 520,000+ community reports region-wide in a year — showing the scale of trust-and-safety operational load needed just to hold fraud near-zero; ~93% of active users never encountered a scammer (implying ~7% did). https://press.carousell.com/2026/04/28/laporan-trend-penipuan-serantau-carousell-2025-mencatatkan-lebih-99-transaksi-bebas-penipuan-meskipun-taktik-penipuan-terus-berkembang/
- OLX/Prosus (FY24/FY25, MEDIUM CONFIDENCE — press release summaries; the primary IR Deep Dive PDF is image-based and unreadable by text extraction, so could not verify granular T&S cost lines): OLX Group revenue +18% YoY to ~$777M with adjusted EBIT +61% to ~$270M (35% margin) in FY25, illustrating that the asset-light classifieds model (vs. inventory models) scales to strong profitability; Prosus explicitly cites AI-driven trust-and-safety/fraud-detection as a cost lever. https://www.prosus.com/news-insights/2025/olx-group-reports-strong-fy-2025-results-with-18-percent-revenue-growth-and-61-percent-profit ; https://www.prosus.com/news-insights/2024/ai-at-prosus-olx-case-study
- The RealReal (US, Q3 2025, HIGH CONFIDENCE — company press release, found via search only after direct URL 404'd): GMV +20% YoY to $520M, revenue +17% to $174M, but net loss widened to $(54)M (31.1% of revenue) from $(18)M a year earlier (much of the delta from a one-off warrant fair-value charge); Adjusted EBITDA margin only 5.4% despite double-digit GMV growth — underscoring how costly authentication-led managed models are to run profitably even at scale. https://investor.therealreal.com/news-releases/news-release-details/realreal-announces-third-quarter-2025-results
- ThredUp (US, FY2025 10-K, HIGH CONFIDENCE — SEC filing): revenue +19.5% to $310.8M, gross margin 79.4%; company shifted to a "primarily consignment" model in 2025 (de-emphasizing owned-inventory "product revenue"), consistent with the broader pattern that consignment/marketplace models outperform owned-inventory resale models. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1484778/000148477826000007/tdup-20251231.htm
- eBay (US, FY2025 10-K, MEDIUM CONFIDENCE — risk-factor language, not a cost figure): explicitly names buyer/seller trust-and-protection programs (Money Back Guarantee, seller protections) as a material cost and litigation/regulatory risk driver. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065088/000106508826000027/ebay-20251231.htm
- Vinted (EU, 2025, MEDIUM CONFIDENCE — trade press, not primary filing): revenue grew from €813M (2024) to ~€1.1B (2025) and stayed profitable despite "heavier investment" — Vinted funds trust/buyer-protection largely via a buyer-side protection fee (3–8% of item price) rather than seller commission, a notable P2P monetization-of-trust model. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinted
- Back Market (global, 2025, MEDIUM CONFIDENCE — aggregator citing company data): GMV €3.5B (+32% YoY), revenue €440M (~12.6% take rate), reached global EBITDA profitability in 2025 without new funding since Jan 2022 — evidence that a marketplace-plus-strict-grading model (not owned inventory) can be a profitable refurb model. https://thebrandhopper.com/featured-startups/back-market-founders-business-model-revenue-streams-funding/

RANKED PROBLEMS BY EVIDENCE STRENGTH AND SIZE
1. (Operator, strong evidence) Inventory/managed-model unit economics are structurally worse than marketplace models — Cars24/Spinny (~80% procurement cost of expenses, sustained 9-figure-Cr losses) vs. CarTrade/OLX/Back Market (asset-light, profitable). Best-evidenced finding in this research.
2. (Operator, strong evidence) Trust-and-safety is a permanent, scaling operational cost even for "safe" platforms — Carousell suspended 451K+ accounts/reviewed 520K+ reports in a year at 99.96% "scam-free"; eBay and OLX both name fraud/T&S as material, recurring cost centers.
3. (Operator, strong evidence) Authentication/managed-consignment models (RealReal) carry high overhead relative to GMV growth — 20% GMV growth still produced only 5.4% Adjusted EBITDA margin and a widened net loss.
4. (Operator, medium evidence) Fee design directly moves GMV — Mercari's flat 10% buyer fee visibly hurt US GMV before a 2025 redesign.
5. (Seller-professional, medium evidence) Cross-platform fee variance (Poshmark ~20% vs eBay ~13%) forces high markups, squeezing reseller margins.
6. (Seller-casual, weak/anecdotal evidence) No-shows, lowballing, and listing effort are widely reported pain points, but I could not find a rigorous, independent, non-vendor quantification — this is the single biggest evidence gap for Track A and worth either primary research (a short seller survey) or citing platform-side data if the hackathon team can get it under NDA.
7. (Seller-professional, weak evidence) Crosslisting time/ROI claims are vendor-sourced and directionally plausible but not independently verified.
