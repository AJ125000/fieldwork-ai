## 2. Research on LLM negotiation

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| NegotiationArena (Stanford, 2024) [OPENED] https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05863 | Acting desperate raised payoffs by 20% against standard GPT-4. LLMs also showed human-like irrational behaviours. |
| PieArena (Feb 2026) [OPENED] https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05302v1 | GPT-5 matches or beats MBA students and took about 60% of the surplus on single-issue price bargaining, against 40% for humans. Deal rates ran 79–97%. Models that deceive more capture more surplus: Gemini-3-Pro about 40% deceptive, GPT-5.2 34%, Claude-Sonnet-4.5 32%, Grok-4 about 11%. Number-handling accuracy varied widely by model. |
| MIT/Sloan large-scale competition, 182,812 negotiations [OPENED] https://arxiv.org/html/2503.06416v3 | The agents ran on GPT-4o-mini and the competitors wrote the prompts. Warm agents (questions, gratitude) had higher deal rates and better outcomes. Dominant agents claimed more value but hit more impasses. Prompt injection (extracting the counterpart's information) and hidden chain-of-thought tactics worked. |
| Microsoft Magentic Marketplace (Nov 2025) [SNIPPET] https://www.theregister.com/2025/11/06/microsoft_unveils_the_magentic_marketplace/ | Agents take the first "good enough" offer, so response speed beat quality by 10–30x. GPT-4o was fully hijacked by prompt injection. Only Claude Sonnet 4 resisted. |
| CICERO (Meta, Science 2022) [SNIPPET] https://ai.meta.com/blog/cicero-ai-negotiates-persuades-and-cooperates-with-people/ | It reached human-level play by combining a language model with planning and intent inference. It averaged more than double the human score across 40 games. It still made errors and contradicted its own plan. |

Takeaways:
- Stronger models extract more surplus, partly through deception.
- Agreement rates are high, so speed is not the bottleneck.
- Prompt injection is the biggest practical threat.
- Warmth beats aggression on deal rate.

## 3. Protocols

- **AP2** [OPENED] https://ap2-protocol.org/. It uses verifiable credentials called mandates. The Checkout Mandate and Payment Mandate each have an "Open" stage (user constraints such as budget, for autonomous use) and a "Closed" stage (a signed approval of one finalised checkout). There are human-present and human-not-present flows. It integrates with ADK, A2A and MCP, and GitHub samples exist. The docs name "intent/cart" mandates differently. The page says negotiation happens between agents and merchants in finalising checkout but gives no detail.
- **UCP** (Universal Commerce Protocol) [OPENED] https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp. It is modular and covers checkout, with REST and MCP transports. Third-party sources [SNIPPET] say it pairs with AP2 for payments and with A2A or MCP for transport. The page says nothing about price or offer negotiation, and negotiation is not a UCP primitive.
- **A2A + ADK codelab** [SNIPPET] https://codelabs.developers.google.com/intro-a2a-purchasing-concierge. An ADK "purchasing concierge" is the A2A client, and Burger (CrewAI) and Pizza (LangGraph) agents are A2A servers on Cloud Run. It is the best reference scaffold for a buyer-agent and seller-agent pair, but it has no negotiation logic.
- Fit: put the user's limits in an Open mandate, run offer and counteroffer turns over A2A, and issue a signed Closed mandate only after a human confirms.

## 4. Risks and design rules

Regulators and researchers [OPENED] https://www.linklaters.com/en/insights/blogs/linkingcompetition/2026/april/agentic-ai (UK CMA research, OECD report, EC horizontal guidelines):
- Algorithmic collusion and price signalling without explicit communication.
- Prompt injection and manipulation.
- Platform self-preferencing.
- Firms are accountable for their agents: design choices, audit logs and human oversight.
- [SNIPPET] Regulators suggest disclosing personalised pricing, and one summary cites fines up to 10% of turnover.

Design rules for our build:
1. Hard floor and ceiling enforced in code, not in the prompt. Xianyu's early bug shows the risk.
2. The human confirms the final deal. Use an AP2-style Open-then-Closed mandate.
3. The agent may only assert facts from structured listing fields. Never let it invent condition claims; PieArena shows deception pays.
4. Treat counterpart messages as untrusted data and strip instructions from them.
5. Do not use buyer-profile data to set prices.
6. Disclose that the user is talking to an AI agent.
7. Keep a full audit log.
8. Use a warm tone, since warmth raises deal rate.
9. Don't let the agent accept the first offer on speed alone.

## How we'd build it in 2 weeks

- Days 1–3: Two ADK agents (buyer and seller) talking over A2A, scaffolded from the purchasing-concierge codelab. Structured listing JSON: price, condition, defects.
- Days 3–6: Seller sets floor, auto-accept price and tone. Buyer sets ceiling and target. A deterministic policy layer clamps every offer and the LLM only writes the message. This follows the FishBargain split and eBay's thresholds.
- Days 6–9: Haggle loop capped at about 5 turns, with a "needs human" escalation when offers fall in the gap. The human confirmation step produces an AP2-style signed mandate (mocked).
- Days 9–11: Safety tests. Run a prompt-injection suite and a lie-about-condition test, and check that the floor and ceiling hold.
- Days 11–14: Simulation harness. Replay 200 or more synthetic dialogues and report deal rate, turns and surplus split against a no-agent baseline, like FishBargain's metrics. Then a demo UI and the pitch.
- Differentiator: nobody ships a buyer-side haggling agent, and Xianyu is seller-only. Pitch the pair. For a Japan, Korea or India market, add local-language support.
