# R1-13 · Dispute-solution competitors

## Brief given to the researcher

Competitive research (today is 26 Sep 2026) for a proposed product: an AI case handler that helps C2C marketplaces resolve buyer–seller disputes (not received, not as described, fake claims, refund abuse). It gathers the listing, listing photos, chat and complaint evidence, checks claims, flags refund-fraud signals, and recommends an outcome with an explanation for a human agent.

Find everything that already does this or something close, in these groups:
1. Online dispute resolution (ODR): eBay's historic Resolution Center and Modria (and the widely cited figure on how many disputes eBay resolves a year), Tyler Technologies' Modria, Youstice, Kleros, Resolve Disputes Online, and ODR in Asia (e.g. Alibaba/Taobao's crowd-judging jury system 大众评审, JD, Shopee's dispute handling).

3. Returns and refund-abuse detection: Signifyd, Riskified, Forter, Sift, Appriss Retail, Loop, Narvar.

6. Hackathon and startup projects on marketplace dispute resolution, return fraud or resale trust (Devpost, Hugging Face, Product Hunt, GitHub).

For each: what it does, who it serves, whether it handles C2C marketplace disputes with multimodal evidence (photos, chat), JAPAC presence, and a source URL. Deliver under 1,000 words: a table, then a gap analysis (what nobody does), and the biggest risk to novelty.

## Gap analysis — what nobody does

1. **No vendor combines all four inputs (listing data + listing photos + chat transcript + complaint text) into one AI evidence-correlation and outcome-recommendation engine for marketplace C2C disputes.** Fraud-scoring vendors (Signifyd/Forter/Sift/Narvar) score *accounts and orders*, not individual *cases* with a written rationale for a human agent. T&S platforms (Cinder, SafetyKit) manage *cases* but don't reason over evidence to recommend an outcome. ODR players (eBay-era, Shopee, Taobao) do case-level adjudication but with human judgment or crowd votes, not an LLM producing an explained recommendation.
2. **No one explicitly targets the "not as described" + fake-claim + refund-abuse quad simultaneously** with a single explainable model — existing tools each cover one slice (chargeback reps, return-fraud scoring, or general moderation).
3. **Nobody has productized image-forensics-plus-chat-plus-listing cross-checking** (e.g., does the buyer's "damaged item" photo match the seller's listing photo; does the chat admit receipt) as an off-the-shelf SaaS — it exists only inside internal platform ops (Shopee, JD, Taobao, eBay) and in one non-production GitHub demo.
4. **Modria's exit from e-commerce back into government/courts (2017) is a meaningful signal** — the team that literally invented this category concluded courts, not marketplaces, were the durable market; nobody has since rebuilt a dedicated AI version for marketplaces.

## Biggest risk to novelty

The real risk isn't a named competitor — it's that **every large marketplace (eBay, Shopee, JD, Taobao, Amazon, and by extension Meesho itself) already runs this exact workflow internally as unglamorous ops tooling**, refined over a decade, and could bolt an LLM onto their existing evidence-collection pipeline faster than an outside vendor could sell in (data-access and trust barriers are the moat, not the AI). Additionally, general fraud/T&S vendors (Sift, Forter, Cinder) are one product-extension away from this: they already ingest order and chat signals for fraud scoring and could add an "explain the recommended case outcome" layer without new infrastructure. The defensible wedge is likely selling to **mid-size/emerging C2C marketplaces that lack eBay/Shopee-scale internal ops teams** and need this as a purchasable module — not displacing platforms that already do it in-house.
