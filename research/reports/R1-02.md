## Full report as delivered

Research on buyer-side second-hand/classifieds problems, JAPAC-focused, compiled from ~13 opened sources (2 PDFs/pages returned 403/unparseable — noted below).

## 1. Scams & fraud (strongest, best-quantified category)

**Singapore (SPF, Annual Scam & Cybercrime Brief 2025, police.gov.sg — primary source PDF; I could not get clean text extraction from the PDF itself, so these figures come from search-engine synthesis of that same document, medium-high confidence):**
E-commerce scams were Singapore's most-reported scam type in 2025: 6,703 cases, S$16.7m lost, avg S$2,503/victim. Carousell = 75.5% of e-commerce scam cases, Facebook = 18.6%. Overall 2025 scam losses fell to S$913.1m (from S$1.1bn in 2024), 41,974 total cases (−24.8%). Source: https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Media-Room/Statistics/Annual-Scams-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025/Annual-Scam-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2025.pdf

**Australia (ACCC/National Anti-Scam Centre, opened directly, high confidence):** Jan–Sep 2025: shopping scams = most-reported scam type with financial loss — 9,628 reports, A$8.6m lost (+19% YoY). Full 2025: ~21,255 shopping-scam reports, ~A$9.4m lost (Jan–Oct). Total all-scam losses trending toward >A$2bn/year. Source: https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/australians-report-nearly-260m-in-losses-as-shopping-scams-surge

**South Korea (Korea Herald/Sedaily, opened, high confidence):** Direct-transaction (secondhand) fraud cases rose from ~80,000 (2021) to ~120,000 (2024/25); losses tripled from ₩257.4bn (~$186m) to ₩874.1bn (~$632m). Consumer-relief claims against platforms: Danggeun Market rose from 3 (2021) to 88 (2024); Bungaejangter from 5 to 57. Source: https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/04/08/secondhand-platform-fraud-complaints-double-as-consumer

**Japan (search synthesis, medium confidence — did not open primary NPA source):** Specialized-fraud losses nearly doubled in 2025 (+98% to ¥142.3bn); Mercari disclosed user data to police in 1,540 of 3,371 requested cases (2H 2025); 7,294 consumer-affairs consultations re: flea-market services in FY2025.

**India (I4C/NCRB, medium confidence — no OLX-specific national count found):** NCRB: cyber-fraud/cheating cases rose from 27,248 (2018) to 101,928 (2024), 73,987 of which were financial fraud. I4C: ~7,000 cybercrime complaints/day (May 2024). OLX/Quikr-specific figures exist only as scattered local cases (e.g., ₹15.74 crore across 4,875 complaints in one state operation) — **no consolidated national OLX-fraud statistic exists in public data.**

**ASEAN region (GSMA "State of Scams in ASEAN" 2025 — page returned 403, so figures below are from search synthesis only, low-medium confidence):** 8% of consumers in ID/MY/PH/SG/TH/VN scammed in past 12 months; 68% of victims lost money; 45% of reported cases unresolved. Philippines reportedly has the highest shopping-scam rate (36%) of 11 Asian countries surveyed (source page 403'd — low confidence, could not verify sample/methodology).

## 2. Item-not-as-described / condition mismatch
No platform publishes a hard dispute-rate percentage publicly. eBay's seller-performance policy confirms SNAD ("not as described") returns are treated as the costliest dispute category (extra 5-6% fee tier for high SNAD sellers) — qualitative confirmation, not a %. Same pattern on Mercari/Vinted (SNAD = most contested claim type) per secondary reseller-tooling sites, not company disclosures. **Quantified frequency data is genuinely missing/undisclosed by Vinted, Mercari, Back Market, Carousell.**

## 3. Trust/purchase-barrier survey data
Academic study (Calvo-Porral et al. 2024, Marketing Intelligence & Planning, n=405, Spain) confirms trust deficit, contamination effects, and low perceived product reliability are the top-ranked barriers — but the paywalled full text withheld exact percentages (medium confidence, abstract-only). OfferUp 2025 Recommerce Report (US, n=1,500) shows stigma declining (70% say stigma has "decreased") but contains no barrier percentages. India: one secondary academic source states ~60% of OLX users cite fraud concern as a barrier (low-medium confidence, could not verify primary study).

## 4. Counterfeits, meetup safety, other
Counterfeit-purchase-rate surveys are wildly inconsistent across sources (Incopro: 26% fooled in past 12mo; MSU: 68%; Red Points 2025: 39% knowing + 15% both) — **flag all as low confidence due to non-comparable methodologies**, and none are secondhand-marketplace-specific. Norton (US, n=1,000, Mar 2025, opened directly): 41% of consumers encountered a social-marketplace scam, 13% became victims (32% of encounters converted); Gen Z encounter rate 60% vs Boomers 25%; clothing (38%) and electronics (24%) most-targeted categories. Meetup safety and payment risk: only qualitative mentions found (Carousell safety-advisory pages); no quantified survey data located for JAPAC. Price-uncertainty/overpaying and discovery-friction: **no quantified data found anywhere in this research** — genuine gap.

## Ranked by strength/breadth of evidence
1. **Classifieds fraud/scams** — strongest: multiple government sources (SG, AU, KR, partial JP/IN), consistent multi-year trend of rising losses and case counts.
2. **Trust as a purchase barrier** — moderate: consistent qualitative finding across academic + industry surveys, but exact percentages mostly locked behind paywalls or undisclosed.
3. **Item-not-as-described disputes** — moderate-weak: universally acknowledged by platforms as the top dispute type, but no platform discloses a rate.
4. **Counterfeit risk** — weak: real but numbers vary 26–68% depending on survey, not secondhand-specific.
5. **Meetup safety / payment risk / overpaying / discovery friction** — weakest: essentially no quantified JAPAC data found; treat as a research gap for the hackathon team to validate qualitatively (interviews) rather than cite stats.
