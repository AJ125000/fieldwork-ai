# R2-08 · Benchmarks and brand resale

## Brief given to the researcher

Research (today is 2 Oct 2026), in two parts.

PART A: the most advanced second-hand platforms as benchmarks. What do they do for trust, disputes, listing, discovery and AI?
- Xianyu (闲鱼, Alibaba): scale (users, GMV), the "Xianyu Small Court" (闲鱼小法庭) crowd-jury dispute system (how it works, volumes, outcomes), credit-based trust (Sesame credit), inspection services (验货宝), AI listing and pricing, AI customer service.
- Rakuma (Rakuten), Jmty (ジモティー, local classifieds), Kream (Korea sneakers, with inspection), Snkrdunk (Japan): their trust and inspection models.
- Any platform using AI for dispute or claim decisions, or for condition grading from photos.
For each: what is novel, scale, and the source.

PART B: brands and retailers doing resale, which is the B2B side of second-hand. Resale-as-a-service vendors (Trove, Archive, Treet, Recurate, Reflaunt in Asia), brand take-back programmes in JAPAC (e.g. Uniqlo Re.Uniqlo, IKEA buy-back in Japan, Singapore and Australia, Apple and Samsung trade-in), returns and liquidation marketplaces (B-Stock), and open-box resale. Market size and growth of brand resale, the problems brands face (grading and condition assessment at scale, pricing, authenticity, logistics), and where AI is used.

Open pages; don't rely on snippets; mark snippet-only items. About 25 fetches max. Return under 1,000 words with tables and URLs, plus gaps. Return findings as text; don't write files.

**Brand pain points** (Trove, Treet, Amazon and vendor sources): inconsistent manual grading, pricing speed, fear of cannibalisation (Trove says most resale buyers are new to the brand), authenticity, and reverse logistics. The sources describe AI in pricing, photo grading, auto-listing and routing, but publish no accuracy data.
