## Observations on the regulatory trend

1. **Direction is unambiguously toward more platform responsibility, not less.** Every JAPAC market surveyed has either passed, amended, or proposed a rule in 2023–2026 that adds platform-side obligations for disputes/scams (Korea's Dec-2025 overhaul, Singapore's OCHA codes and TSR, Australia's SPF, Philippines IRR, India's dark-patterns layer). None found relaxing prior duties.

2. **C2C/secondhand platforms are the newest, most contested frontier.** Korea is the clearest case: its 2024–2025 E-Commerce Act amendments are the first APAC law to explicitly write peer-to-peer trade into e-commerce regulation with a two-tier (platform-first, then KISA) dispute process — a direct regulatory response to Karrot/Bunjang/Joonggonara volume. By contrast, the Philippines' ITA and India's E-Commerce Rules both stop short of C2C, and Japan's DPF law only bites C2C sellers who behave "like a business" — showing real regulatory uncertainty about where to draw the C2C line.

3. **"Soft" reputational levers are filling the gap before hard law catches up.** Singapore's Transaction Safety Ratings publicly score Carousell below B2C rivals specifically on dispute/verification criteria, and its OCHA E-Commerce Code turns some of that into binding duties (seller ID verification, escrow-style payment protection) — a two-step model (rate, then mandate) other regulators may copy.

4. **Timelines and enforcement teeth are tightening.** Where older rules used vague "best efforts" language (Japan's 努力義務), newer instruments specify hard clocks (India's 48-hour/1-month rule, Korea's tiered escalation to KISA) and real penalties (Australia's AUD $50m/offence, Singapore's criminal offence for non-compliance with a Rectification Notice).

5. **An ombudsman/external-dispute-resolution model is emerging as the likely next benchmark**, championed by the ACCC (83% public support) and structurally built into Australia's SPF and Korea's KISA-escalation step — but Australia has deliberately excluded marketplaces from its first tranche, so the "designation" question (will marketplaces be pulled in like banks/telcos?) is the single biggest near-term swing factor for the region, expected to resolve around the 1 July 2026 SPF sector-designation date.
