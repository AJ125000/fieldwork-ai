# R2-06 · Category trust problems

## Brief given to the researcher

Research (today is 2 Oct 2026): how trust and transaction problems differ by CATEGORY in second-hand commerce, focusing on JAPAC (India, Southeast Asia, Japan, Korea, Australia). For each category give the category's size or share of second-hand trade where available, its specific failure modes, what checks exist today, and where AI could help.

Categories:
1. Smartphones and electronics: stolen phones and IMEI blacklists (India's CEIR portal, Sanchar Saathi), iCloud or Google lock, battery health, refurbished-grade confusion, fake parts.
2. Two-wheelers and cars (India especially): odometer tampering, accident or flood history, RC and ownership transfer delays, loans on the vehicle (hypothecation), challans; tools like Vahan and Parivahan checks.
3. Fashion and footwear: authenticity, hygiene and smell, sizing and fit, colour mismatch in photos.
4. Furniture and home appliances (relocation and moving-out sales): transport and logistics cost, size and measurement, hidden damage, appliance working condition, disposal.
5. Collectibles and trading cards (Japan, Korea): grading (PSA, BGS), counterfeits, condition subtleties.
6. Kids' items and books: briefly.

For each: evidence with sources (surveys, government data, platform help pages, news), and which category has the highest trust burden per transaction. Open pages; don't rely on snippets; mark snippet-only items. About 20 fetches max. Return under 1,000 words: a comparison table plus short notes per category, URLs, and gaps. Return findings as text; don't write files.

**Notes by category**

1. **Phones.** The CEIR site shows 6.08M phones blocked, 3.78M traced and 1.40M recovered (37.0% recovery; Delhi only 8.1%, Lakshadweep 83.3%). The SMS check "KYM <IMEI>" to 14422 or the web portal returns manufacturer and model data only, so it is a first-stage screen. Sanchar Saathi recovered over 50,000 phones in October 2025 and over 700,000 since launch (Tribune, opened). The government has required IMEI disclosure before sale [S]. Grading is not standardised across sellers, and 80% battery health is a common refurbisher minimum [S, refurbisher blogs]. Singapore police e-commerce scam cases show that phone scams are mostly non-delivery after a deposit.
   - Gap: I found no sourced rate for how many second-hand phones are blacklisted. I also found nothing that quantifies iCloud lock or fake-part incidents.

2. **Cars and two-wheelers.** Spinny's RC-transfer guide (opened) says the buyer applies within 14 days for Delhi-registered cars and 45 days for out-of-state cars, with ₹500 a month in penalties. The seller files Form 29 and the buyer files Form 30. A live loan needs financier consent, or Form 35 plus the lender's NOC if the loan is repaid. Pending challans or a blacklist entry can block transfer. An incomplete transfer leaves the seller liable and causes insurance and resale problems for the buyer.
   - Gap: "1 in 3 unorganised cars have rolled-back meters" is an unsourced industry estimate on a [W] page (vahanbazaar). Treat it as unverified. I found no government or academic odometer-fraud rate. CRISIL's page has no fraud data either.

3. **Fashion.** Mercari's "Anshin Kantei" authentication started in March 2024 and covers sneakers, bags and trading cards. IVA's FAKEBUSTERS covers 88 brands, claims 99.9% accuracy and has appraised 1.5M items (valueaddedresource, opened). Mercari invested in IVA, a $5.8M round. Hygiene, smell and sizing have no sourced data.
   - FICCI CASCADE's counterfeit-apparel figure of upwards of $4bn [S] is a counterfeiting estimate, not second-hand-specific.

4. **Furniture and appliances.** I found no usable JAPAC data. One 2014-era OLX study said 82% of respondents saw higher seller trust on online marketplaces because they can meet the seller and test the item [S, old]. This is the biggest evidence gap, and the category is likely logistics-bound rather than fraud-bound.

5. **Collectibles.**
   - **Counterfeit prevalence:** a 2023 SNKRDUNK survey says 59.3% of Japanese card buyers met counterfeit or repackaged cards [S, single survey, treat cautiously].
   - **Counterfeit slabs and mis-graded cards:** forum threads report fake PSA slabs and PSA grading fakes (elitefourum [S], anecdotal).
   - **Mercari measures:** Mercari-Pokémon partnership (June 2023, opened via forum summary); Mercari limits listings of Pokémon 30th-anniversary merchandise [S]. The Japanese government has also formed a task force on card scalping and fakes (Dexerto [S]).
   - **Grading firms:** PSA, BGS and ARS (Japan). ARS grades condition only, not centring [S].
   - **Korea:** I found no Korea-specific card data.

6. **Kids' items and books.** Little evidence either way.

**Cross-region context**
- **Japan:** recommerce was $5.59bn in 2024 and $6.16bn in 2025, growing to $8.5bn by 2029. Categories include apparel, electronics, appliances, home decor, books/toys and auto parts. The report says electronics and fashion are "consolidating" (GlobeNewswire, opened; the Chiba authentication centre is Mercari's).
- **Australia:** recommerce $4.2bn in 2024, with a forecast of $6.75bn by 2029 [S]. The ACCC notes marketplace scams, mainly PayID overpayment tricks aimed at sellers [S].
- **Korea:** police-recorded second-hand fraud cases ran 78,320 in 2023 (₩137.3bn) (fnnews, opened). A search snippet [S] gives 100,539 cases and ₩334bn for 2024, and 81,252 cases for January–October 2025. THE CHEAT's private tracker logged 312,321 cases in 2023 (the fnnews article text says "31 million", which looks like a typo; the snippet says 312,321). There is no item-type breakdown, though gift cards are flagged as an emerging scam.
- **Singapore:** e-commerce scams were the most reported scam type in 2025: 6,703 cases, S$16.7m lost, ~S$2,503 average [S]. Carousell cases rose 17.1% to 1,555 in H1 2026 [S]. I could not open the SPF brief to confirm.
- **Not opened:** the Malay Mail page returned 403, so the Singapore figures are snippet-only.

**Which category has the highest trust burden per transaction**
Cars and two-wheelers. A mistake there costs the most, and the verification chain is long: identity, title, loan, challans, mileage and accident history. Trading cards have the highest burden per card, but the trade is small. Phones have the highest transaction frequency and most documented scam volume in SEA.

**Gaps**
- No platform-neutral per-category trust or complaint dataset exists; I found none for any JAPAC country. A consumer survey ranking categories by trust is the missing piece.
- Furniture and appliances, kids' items and books have almost no sourced evidence.
- Counterpoint and Japan's environment ministry (env.go.jp) category PDFs were paywalled or unreadable.
- Odometer and flood-fraud rates are unsourced.
