## 2. Polls
No genuine native X or LinkedIn poll (with visible vote-percentage splits) on marketplace scams/disputes turned up in the searched items — this is a real gap, not an omission. What did surface instead were third-party survey reports frequently cited on LinkedIn/in press, which are NOT polls but are the closest quantified sentiment data found:
- BrandShield 2026 Consumer Fraud & Trust Report (1,029 US adults): 52.4% targeted by a scam in the past 12 months; TikTok Shop most-cited platform for marketplace fraud/counterfeits (34.7%), followed by eBay, Craigslist, Etsy, Poshmark; 79.5% say a scam changed their shopping behavior (28.6% now buy only from trusted sellers, 27.6% now verify sellers first, 10.6% left the platform entirely). (https://ppc.land/tiktok-shop-cited-most-for-marketplace-fraud-by-34-7-of-us-shoppers/)
- Norton research: 41% of respondents encountered a social-marketplace scam; ~32% of those who encountered one fell victim (≈13% of all consumers). (https://us.norton.com/blog/research/marketplace-scam-statistics)
These are survey results embedded in shareable articles, not interactive polls — flagged as a limit below.

## 3. Operator-side insights (what T&S/fraud/support professionals say is hard or costly)
- Chargeback/dispute reviewers process very high volume with little time per case (~20 cases/hour, ~2 minutes each per one payments professional's post), so evidence must be extremely concise and front-loaded — poorly structured seller evidence loses by default.
- A fraud-prevention industry post frames refund/promo abuse as the single biggest threat category to online marketplaces, ahead of classic account-takeover fraud.
- One fraud-tech data point (cited in a LinkedIn article): 66% of merchants feel pressured to issue refunds even when they have evidence the buyer is misrepresenting the item's condition — i.e., dispute processes are tilted toward automatic buyer wins, which operators say invites gaming.
- Returns/return-fraud industry figures put the annual cost of return fraud and abuse at roughly $101B (up from $85B the prior year per a 2023 report), reflecting how "item not as described" claims are increasingly used as a return-abuse vector rather than a genuine defect complaint.
- Trust & Safety commentary on large marketplaces emphasizes a speed-vs-fairness tension: AI/ML listing and seller-vetting systems need human review before action, which is itself a scaling bottleneck.
- Regional/operator data (Korea Herald, citing platform-level complaint counts) shows complaint volume against a single platform (Karrot) rising from 3 to 88 in a few years even as transaction volume also scaled — operators frame this as fraud growing faster than trust-and-safety headcount.

## 4. Honest limits
- No verifiable, view-count-confirmed native X/LinkedIn poll on this exact topic was found; the "polls" section above substitutes third-party survey data, clearly labeled as such.
- Most X/TikTok/Facebook items came back as search snippets only — actual like/view/share counts were frequently not exposed by search results and are marked "not visible" rather than guessed.
- Coverage skews toward Singapore/Malaysia (rich English-language police/news coverage) and US/global generic content; direct social posts specifically from Indonesia, Philippines, Japan, and India in JAPAC were thin — most India/Indonesia/Philippines material found was advisory/blog content rather than individual viral posts, and Japan-specific marketplace-scam social content did not surface in the searches run.
- LinkedIn/X post text often could not be fully read (login walls); descriptions rely on search-snippet text and, for two posts, a direct fetch — engagement figures beyond the one post fetched (13 reactions) are unconfirmed.
- Per the privacy instruction, no private individuals' names or handles appear in this write-up's prose even where they appeared in source URLs or snippets; LinkedIn professionals are identified only by role/company type.
- Roughly 20 WebSearch queries and 4 WebFetch calls were used; did not exhaustively check every named platform (e.g., no distinct items surfaced specifically for Vinted-in-JAPAC, since Vinted has minimal JAPAC presence, or for Cashify beyond consumer-complaint sites rather than social posts).
