**Date:** 8 Oct 2026 · **Reads:** ROADMAP-APPAREL-v2.md, ROADMAP-APPAREL.md (Stage B, Stage E, section 7)
**Scope:** will the numbers we show judges be credible or embarrassing? Severity: **H** = a sharp judge can sink the claim in one question; **M** = weakens it; **L** = nitpick.

## 0. Facts checked on the web (so the attacks are grounded)

| Check | What I found | So what |
|---|---|---|
| **ConfLVLM** (EMNLP 2025, arXiv 2502.20560) | The 87.8% to 10.0% result is **LLaVA-1.5 scene descriptions**, with claim labels from **GPT-4o** (humans checked only 50 images / 1,182 claims, ICC 0.85). Main experiments use **400 calibration responses, 100 test, 50 random splits**; the calibration unit is the **whole response (image)**, not the claim. The guarantee is **marginal**, assumes exchangeability, and the paper notes abstention can get high. No code release stated in what I could read. | Our 40 garments are 10x smaller than their calibration set. LLaVA-1.5 is a very weak base model, so 88% to 10% will not transfer to Gemini. Do not quote it as our result. |
| **Follow-up (arXiv 2606.16667, "Look Again Before You Abstain")** | Says a conformal grounding filter must abstain on **more than 80%** of claims to hold the error rate below 5% on a balanced object-existence benchmark. Proposes crop and zoom to acquire evidence instead of abstaining. | A judge who knows this will ask our abstention rate. It also supports our Agentic Vision loop: cite it as motivation. |
| **Trust or Escalate** (ICLR 2025, arXiv 2407.18370) | Guarantee: P(judge = human \| confidence >= lambda) >= 1 - alpha, holding with probability 1 - delta over the calibration draw. Fixed-sequence testing with an exact binomial upper bound, **500 calibration instances** with **human labels**, delta 0.1, i.i.d. assumption. At a 90% target under shift, **coverage fell to 31.8%**. The authors say the guarantee is only as good as the human labels. | We have no human-labelled disputes (about 20 synthetic ones). We cannot claim this guarantee. We can claim "same selective rule, threshold unvalidated". |
| **CORN** (Shi, Cao, Raschka, arXiv 2111.08851) | Rank-consistent ordinal heads, official PyTorch repo. The paper's claim is better than CORAL. | Safe to use. The ordinal head is not the risk. Do not market it as an innovation. |
| **Synthetic defects** | Generative defect papers (RoadFusion, TF-IDG, DH-Diff) report gains on their own benchmarks and name two failure modes: defects inconsistent with the background, and mask-image entanglement. My search found **no standard protocol against leakage between synthetic train and synthetic test**. | The circularity is ours to fix. No published precedent protects us. |
| **"First baseline"** | Searching found no published condition or pilling baseline on the RISE/Wargon dataset. Closest is Tabernik and Skocaj (ERK 2025): binary defect detection on the data, SuperSimpleNet AP 72. **The dataset I found is Swedish (RISE, Wargon Innovation, about 31.6k items, expert sorter labels on an ISO 1 to 5 scale). The roadmap calls the cross-domain set "Norway 16.5k". Verify the name and provenance before it goes on a slide.** | The claim is probably true but weak. See row 4. |

## 1. Claim-by-claim attack table

| 1 | Train on front-only sorting-line photos (background removed), test on phone photos; "domain adaptation on 40 garments" | (a) Sorting-line images are cut-out, uniform, single front view. Phone photos have a bed, a floor, hangers, mixed light. Any score on the HF test split says nothing about phone photos. (b) The 40 garments are used for calibration **and** adaptation **and** the human ceiling **and** the demo. That is triple dipping on n = 40. (d) Prior shift: Swedish donation stream vs a marketplace listing mix. (e) Frozen SigLIP embeds brand, colour and garment type; the model may predict grade from brand or category, not wear. | **H** | Report **two separate scores**: in-domain (HF test) and **phone-domain (own garments, labelled "pilot, n = 40")**. Add shortcut baselines: category + brand + colour only, and majority class; if the shortcut baseline is within 0.05 QWK, say so. Say "image-only ceiling is below sorter label ceiling". (d) Claims within a garment are correlated; if you calibrate **per claim** and split claims randomly across cal/test, you get leakage. ## 2. The four claims most likely to backfire

1. **Synthetic defects as both training and test bed (rows 6, 13).** Circular and the easiest thing for a judge to spot. Fix: real physical planted changes as the headline; synthetic only as a labelled stress test; train and test generators disjoint; training benefit shown on real defects only.
2. **"Certified false-match rate" (row 10).** 60 to 100 negatives give about 3 to 5% at best, and the near-identical subset (about 10) gives 26%. Fix: report per stratum with Clopper-Pearson, add 30 hard negatives, and drop "certified".
3. **Conformal and Trust-or-Escalate "guarantees" on 40 garments and 20 synthetic disputes (rows 7, 8, 9).** The papers use 400 to 500 labelled calibration items. Fix: calibrate in-domain on 1.7k HF items, report phone-domain coverage empirically, call the claim filter "after ConfLVLM", drop "guaranteed" for disputes.
4. **Human column and "first baseline" (rows 2, 4).** There is no human inter-rater number in the data, and "first" is a soft claim. Fix: relabel 150 test images with two raters; word "first" as "to our knowledge".

**Presentation rules**
- Every row shows N and its interval in the cell. Anything under N = 100 is marked "pilot".
- Synthetic-bed numbers never appear in the headline slide.
- Wording bans: "certified rate", "guaranteed" (outside the in-domain conformal row), "first" without "to our knowledge", "human" for a non-expert rater.
- One honest losing row (e.g., Gemini better on phone-domain grade) raises credibility more than any win.
